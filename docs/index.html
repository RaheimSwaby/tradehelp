What's inside

Local-first. Your trades never leave your machine.

Everything lives in a SQLite database on your computer. No cloud account, no server, no data leaving your machine. You own your journal the same way you own a spreadsheet, except it's actually built for traders.

Offline AI coach that runs on your hardware.

Connect your own Ollama or LM Studio instance, or bring your own API key. The AI reviews your trades, spots patterns, and gives you a coaching report, all without sending your data anywhere. It's like having a second set of eyes on every session.

Import from anywhere. CSV auto-detect does the work.

Drop in a CSV from NinjaTrader, Tradovate, or TopstepX and TradeHelp figures out the columns. No manual mapping, no reformatting. Just drag, drop, and your trade history is in.

Prop-firm guardrails that actually understand prop rules.

Set per-account profit targets, trailing drawdown cushions, and consistency rules. TradeHelp tracks each account separately and warns you before you breach. Built by someone who's blown accounts and learned the hard way.

Live guardrails while you trade.

Pre-trade checklist, max-loss alarm, and daily loss limit, all running locally so there's zero latency. No API calls, no internet required. Just a safety net between you and a bad decision.

Why I built TradeHelp

I built TradeHelp because I did not want to keep paying a subscription.

When I started trading five years ago I made a lot of mistakes, and it never occurred to me to look at myself as the problem. I would switch up the strategy, or blame the market, when what I actually lacked was discipline. It took some experience before I understood that the market was not the issue. I was.

I did not have much money starting out, so the bigger cloud journals like TradeZella and TraderSync were out of reach. I would go months at breakeven, and I could not justify paying hundreds a year for software to tell me that.

So the goal here is simple: give smaller traders real insight into their own habits without charging them every month for it.

On the engineering side, I was a software engineer at Amazon, where I worked on several projects and shipped features to production. TradeHelp is not perfect and it is still a work in progress, but if any of the above sounds familiar, give it a shot.
